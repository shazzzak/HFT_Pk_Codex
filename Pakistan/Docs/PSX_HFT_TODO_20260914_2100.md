## D. Cross-asset and futures research (new, 2026-09-13)

### 19. Futures → spot lead-lag

For every name with a listed single-stock future, establish which leg
price-discovers first. If the future leads, its mid becomes a predictive input
for the imminent move in the underlying, and the maker can pull or reprice the
threatened side before the spot book reflects it.

**Method, production form:**
- Signal on **mid or microprice**, never trade prices — bid-ask bounce induces
  negative autocorrelation that contaminates any cross-correlation estimate.
- Use **Hayashi-Yoshida** covariance or event-time alignment. Naive resampled
  cross-correlation is invalid here: the more actively quoted instrument
  mechanically appears to lead (Epps effect). On PSX, futures are usually the
  thinner leg, so a naive estimator will under-state futures leadership; on a
  name where the future is the busier leg it will over-state it. Either way the
  bias runs with liquidity, not with information.
- Control for the **market factor** (index or a basket). Without it, everything
  appears to lead everything, because the common component moves both.
- **Decision-grade test is not correlation.** Run the candidate signal through
  the existing markout lens: does conditioning a quote on Δfuture improve the
  realised markout of fills in the spot name? A lead-lag coefficient that does
  not survive that test is not tradeable.
- Establish a **horizon**: leadership at 100 ms and leadership at 5 s are
  different claims with different exploitability given our latency.
- **Walk-forward it** the same way the config assignment was validated
  (split-half, sign stability, 40/60/98-day warm-ups). Lead-lag relationships
  decay and leadership can switch.

**Prerequisite:** confirm the futures feed exists in the parsed store at the same
event granularity as the equity feed, with the same timestamp source. Two feeds
with different clock discipline will manufacture a lead-lag that is pure clock
skew. This is the first thing to check — before any modelling.

### 20. Sector-leader lead-lag

Same machinery, applied within sectors (cement, banks, textile, fertiliser, E&P,
power, tech). Find the name, if any, whose mid changes precede the rest of its
group, and use it as a cross-name signal — e.g. if UBL leads the banks, a UBL mid
move becomes an input to the quote in MCB, HBL, BAFL, MEBL, NBP, BOP, AKBL, BAHL,
HMB, FABL.

**Notes:**
- `leadlag_screen.py` already exists and is validated but has **never been run**;
  its sector map is incomplete. Completing the map is the first task.
- "Largest free-float market cap or largest traded value leads" is a **prior, not
  a finding.** Test it. Record how often it holds — on PSX the largest name is
  often the most index-driven, which can make it the *follower* of sector news
  that surfaces first in a mid-cap with concentrated informed flow.
- The same four traps apply: mid not trade price, Hayashi-Yoshida or event-time,
  market-factor control, and markout as the acceptance test.
- Multiple-testing discipline: with ~7 sectors × ~10 names there are hundreds of
  candidate pairs. Pre-register the leader hypothesis per sector (largest
  free-float) and test *that*, rather than mining the full pair matrix and
  reporting the best t-statistic.
- A confirmed leader feeds the throttle, not fair value. The sweep already proved
  that leaning fair value into an imbalance signal is destructive
  (`use_microprice` was removed as an axis for exactly this reason). Default
  assumption: use the signal to **pull the threatened side**, not to shift the
  reservation price.

### 21. Basis-aware market maker

For names with a liquid listed future, run the two books as one strategy:
provide liquidity passively in the futures leg (wider spreads, different fee
schedule) and hedge aggressively in spot, holding a delta-neutral position and
realising the basis.

**The trade as described:**
1. Post passively in futures, get filled short.
2. Immediately buy the same quantity in spot with an aggressive order.
3. Hold the delta-neutral pair.
4. Exit at expiry on convergence, or close both legs early if the basis collapses.

**Corrections that must be built in — see the accompanying analysis. In short:**

- **The futures premium is mostly financing cost, not arbitrage profit.** Fair
  basis ≈ S × (r − q) × T/365. Short future + long spot is a synthetic loan: you
  earn the implied repo rate. The tradeable edge is **basis minus fair carry**,
  not basis. Model and log both, never quote off raw basis.
- **Delta-neutral is not cash-flow-neutral.** The futures leg is margined daily; a
  rally against the short generates variation-margin calls while the spot gain
  sits unrealised. Size against funded margin capacity, with an explicit margin
  model and a stress path.
- **There is a naked window between the legs.** Passive fill, then aggressive
  hedge, means unhedged short exposure for the round-trip latency. Measure that
  window's adverse selection with the markout machinery already built; it is a
  direct deduction from the basis.
- **Convergence is guaranteed only at expiry.** Basis can widen substantially
  first. An early close takes the basis where it is, so the strategy needs a
  holding-period and margin model, not just an entry rule.
- **Cost the four legs.** Passive futures entry, aggressive spot entry (pay the
  spread), then delivery or two closing legs, TREC fees on each. Compare the
  all-in net against the MM book's current 3.316 bps per turn on the same
  capital before committing to it.
- **Dividends move fair basis.** Any cash dividend inside the contract period
  shifts it. Route this through the corporate-action detection built this session
  rather than a separate path.
- **Data first.** Before modelling: which PSX names have listed futures, what are
  the contract specs (deliverable vs cash-settled, expiry convention, margin
  regime, position limits), is the futures feed in the parsed store at equity
  granularity, and what does realised futures liquidity actually look like
  per name per day. Nothing above is worth building until that table exists.
