## D. Cross-asset and futures research

**SCOPE COLLAPSED 2026-09-15.** `probe_futures_width.py` established that PSX
single-stock futures are almost entirely dead. Of 100 roots with an active-month
deliverable contract, only 13 trade more than 1,000 times a day; MCB trades 12,
NATF 10. Spread in bps is almost purely a function of that deadness: Spearman
ρ = −0.875 between trades/day and spread, n = 100, p = 1.3e-32.

19. **Futures → spot lead-lag. LARGELY DEAD.** A lead-lag signal extracted from a
    contract that trades 12 times a day is not a signal. This item survives only
    for the ~13 names with real futures activity (BOP, MLCF, TRG, PAEL, PTC,
    DGKC, TPL, TPLP, KOSM, PIBTL, SSGC, NCPL, PACE). The methodology notes below
    still hold for those.
    - Signal on **mid or microprice**, never trade prices.
    - **Hayashi-Yoshida** or event-time alignment; naive resampled
      cross-correlation is invalid (Epps effect biases with liquidity, not
      information).
    - Control for the **market factor**.
    - **Acceptance test is markout, not correlation.**
    - Establish a **horizon**; 100 ms and 5 s are different claims.
    - **Walk-forward it.**
    - Prerequisite (now partly answered): the futures feed IS in the parsed store
      at equity granularity, same market/phase vocabulary, same `transact_time`
      clock. Snapshot shape verified: 28.7 rows per `orig_time`, 99.4% two-sided.

20. **Sector-leader lead-lag. ALIVE AND CHEAPEST ITEM ON THE LIST.** Uses spot
    only, so none of the futures collapse touches it. `leadlag_screen.py` is built,
    validated on synthetic ground truth (recovered a +1000 ms peak against a true
    +800 ms; corr +0.464 vs +0.155 at lag 0), and the sector map is now complete:
    **107 names, 19 sectors, 6 singletons**, from the exchange's own daily
    quotation sheet. **It has still never been run.**
    - "Largest free-float market cap leads" is a **prior, not a finding.** Test it.
    - Same four traps as item 19.
    - Multiple-testing discipline: pre-register the leader hypothesis per sector
      rather than mining the full pair matrix.
    - A confirmed leader feeds the **throttle**, not fair value. Leaning fair value
      into an imbalance signal is already proven destructive.

21. **Basis-aware market maker. PREMISE UNDERMINED.** The design was: post
    passively in futures, hedge aggressively in spot. Two 2026-09-15 results cut
    against it:
    - The four-arm futures MM run: all four arms lose. Markout is 1.19–1.28× capture
      in every arm. Capture stayed positive throughout, so the A.2 failure mode did
      NOT occur — the failure is simpler, the adverse move after each trade exceeds
      the spread earned. Only the size throttle helped (3/3 names, +3,323 PKR).
    - Even at its nine-month average (+24 PKR/name-day vs October's −392) futures MM
      would run at ~24 PKR/name-day against ~597 for the share book.
    The carry corrections still stand if it is ever revisited: fair basis
    ≈ S × (r − q) × T/365 and the edge is basis **minus** fair carry; delta-neutral
    is not cash-flow-neutral (daily variation margin); there is a naked window
    between the legs; convergence is guaranteed only at expiry; four legs of cost;
    dividends move fair basis.

22. **Delta-hedge ladder. FIRST RUNG INVERTED; DESIGN NOW RESTS ON NETTING.**
    See `PSX_HEDGE_COST_FINDING_20260915_0700.md`. Summary:
    - Crossing the **share book** is cheaper than crossing the same-ticker
      active-month **future** on **99 of 100 names**, median 3.30×. The stated
      first preference is the most expensive rung.
    - **No name clears the 2.67 bps edge by crossing in either book.** Best is
      EFERT shares at 5.30 bps = 2.0× the edge; median share book 13.79 bps =
      5.2×; median futures 48.09 bps = 18.0×.
    - This is structural: a maker **earns** the spread by posting, a hedger
      **pays** it by crossing, and the edge is derived from that same spread. A
      one-for-one delta hedge by crossing cannot be profitable anywhere.
    - **Netting is now load-bearing.** At a budget of 20% of the edge, the median
      name can hedge only 3.9% of traded notional; BOP 7.4%; EFERT 10.1%;
      median futures 1.1%. The median name needs ~96% netting to afford a hedge.
    - **OPEN — the decisive measurement:** what does the 113-name book actually net
      down to? Computable from existing backtest position paths, no new data.
      Nothing else in this item is worth doing first.
    - **OPEN — ETF rung, gated by a screen that costs nothing:** PSX tick is flat
      0.01 PKR, so a one-tick spread is 100/P bps. An instrument under **37.45 PKR**
      cannot have a spread under 2.67 bps; under **89.6 PKR** it cannot have a round
      trip inside the edge including the 1.554 bps spot fees. Apply that to
      NITGETF, UBLPETF, NBPGETF, MZNPETF, MIIETF, JSMFETF, ACIETF, JSGBETF,
      HBLTETF before measuring anything.
    - **OPEN — not a data question:** does PSX restrict cash-market short selling?
      If so the future is not the dearer hedge on the short side, it is the only
      one, and the whole comparison binds on the long side alone.
